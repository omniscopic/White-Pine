The Idea

Dr. Rich Blundell proposes a wooden surfboard residency at the Art Complex Museum.

Everyone needs a home. This project is a celebration of home through love of local trees, wood, water and an appreciation of creative craft.

Locally sourced New England white pine becomes hollow wooden surfboards, built in the open. Young people learn creativity and craftsmanship in a community build. Music from the region's emerging artists carries the stoke.

It is a homecoming, and a return with the elixir: a celebration of the beauty of wood, the places we come from, and positive vibes for the future.

The Build

Every board is built by hand, from the tree up.

Hollow wooden surfboards, built in the open at the museum, from rough-sawn plank to finished board. No factory, no molds, no shortcuts. Just a design, a stack of wood, and a plane.

Who Could Be In It

One proposed host, and a circle of potential partners.

None of these partnerships are in place yet. These are the people Rich hopes to talk with.

Proposed host

Art Complex Museum

The ACM has expressed enthusiasm to host. Home of the residency: a place to build, show the work in progress, and gather the community.

Potential partner

High School Wood Arts

Students could build a real surfboard alongside Rich, from rough-sawn plank to finished board. Duxbury High School's wood arts program is a natural first fit.

Potential partner

Levitate Music Festival

The emerging artists program could bring the soundtrack. Finished boards could go on display in Levitate shops.

Potential partner

White Pine Source

A local landowner, land trust, or sawyer with a New England white pine to give the project. One tree, milled near where it grew.

Shape of the Residency

Eight months, in person as the work needs it.

An 8-month artist residency. Rich is in physical residence only as necessary, in short stays that follow the wood from tree to exhibition.

Proposed timeline

  1. Fall

The Builder

The Provenance of Rich

Rich is an ecologist, natural historian, and scientist-in-residence at the Maria Mitchell Association on Nantucket. Duxbury, Massachusetts is his home habitat, and he is a Cape Cod and Islands organism.

He has been building hollow wooden surfboards for twenty years. His designs and build-methods are used all over the world.
